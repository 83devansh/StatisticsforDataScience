# Practical 5: Probability and Hypothesis Testing

## Objective
To calculate probabilities using the TeachingRatings dataset and perform a two-tailed hypothesis test using the normal distribution.

## Q1. Probability of Receiving an Evaluation Score Greater Than 4.5

- Total observations: 463
- Scores greater than 4.5: 79
- Probability: 0.1706

**Conclusion:** The probability of receiving an evaluation score greater than 4.5 is 0.1706, which means there is approximately a 17.06% chance.

## Q2. Probability of Evaluation Score Greater Than 3.5 and Less Than 4.2

- Total scores in the given range: 195
- Probability: 0.4212

**Conclusion:** The probability of receiving an evaluation score greater than 3.5 and less than 4.2 is 0.4212, which means there is approximately a 42.12% chance.

## Q3. Two-Tailed Test Using Normal Distribution

Given:
- Population mean (μ) = 12
- Population standard deviation (σ) = 5.5
- Sample size (n) = 36
- Sample mean = 10.7
- Significance level (α) = 0.05

Results:
- Standard Error = 0.9167
- Z-score = -1.4182
- P-value = 0.1561

**Conclusion:** Since the p-value (0.1561) is greater than the significance level (0.05), we fail to reject the null hypothesis. Therefore, there is insufficient evidence to conclude that the mean points scored by regional players is different from the historic mean of 12 points per game.
